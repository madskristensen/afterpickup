  - slug: kids-entry-cubbies
    heading: A cubby for each kid
    text: >-
      When we remodeled, we built a cubby for each of the kids right inside
      the front door, with hooks for coats, a spot for the backpack, a drawer
      under the bench for shoes, and a pocket organizer on the door for gloves
      and the smaller things. After school they empty the lunch box, pull out
      school work, put the backpack in the cubby, the shoes in the drawer, and
      the coat on a hook.
  - slug: snack-cabinet
    heading: Snacks they can reach
    text: >-
      After pickup used to start with someone asking us to get a snack down,
      so the snacks they like now live in a low cabinet they can reach, next
      to a small fridge of water, milk, snack cheeses, salami, and bubbly
      water. If it is in that cabinet or that fridge, they can take it, and
      we restock what disappears.
  - slug: craft-closet
    heading: The craft closet
    text: >-
      The craft supplies used to live wherever a grown-up remembered, so we
      hung a clear pocket organizer on the back of a closet door, stacked
      plastic drawers underneath, and started calling it the craft closet.
      The kids can reach the glue, markers, and little bits without waiting
      on us, and that door is where the making starts.
  - slug: craft-mats
    heading: Mats for the dining table
    text: >-
      After the remodel, the old crafts table would not fit, so paint and
      glue moved to the dining table, which still has to be ready for dinner.
      Craft mats protect the wood and lift away in one piece, and they are
      stiff enough to carry a Lego build without the bricks spilling.
  - slug: fort-building-bin
    heading: A bin for forts
    text: >-
      All the kids love building forts, so we keep one bin with two king-size
      sheets and clamps in a few sizes. The sheets are light enough to cover
      chairs and cushions, the clamps hold them pulled tight, and the kids
      can build with little help from us.
---

The first minutes after the kids come through the door used to be coats on the floor, a snack we had to fetch, and a hunt for the glue. We gave each of those a spot the kids can reach, starting just inside the front door, so the afternoon can get going without one of us standing in the middle of it.
