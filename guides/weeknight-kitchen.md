  - slug: a-quarter-inch-of-water
    heading: Eggs in a quarter inch of water
    text: >-
      Mads's dad has made eggs this way since Mads was a kid: about a quarter
      inch of water, a lid, and a timer, and the eggs do not even have to
      touch the water because the steam under the lid does the cooking. After
      pickup they are usually done before anyone has found the salt.
  - slug: cold-oven-bacon
    heading: Bacon in a cold oven
    text: >-
      Thick bacon goes on a foil-lined sheet into a cold oven set to 400,
      which leaves time for a shower before we look, about fifteen or twenty
      minutes later, and often closer to twenty-five before it is done. We
      pull it when it is just starting to look a little brown, move the strips
      onto paper towel, and they come out crisp outside with a soft bite still
      in the middle.
  - slug: thaw-ground-beef
    heading: Warm water for a forgotten pound
    text: >-
      Some nights we get home and nothing is out for dinner, so a sealed pound
      of ground beef goes into a bowl of warm tap water, not hot, and about
      thirty minutes later it has softened through. We keep the plastic closed
      so the water stays outside the meat, and then it can be tacos or a
      skillet we already know how to make.
  - slug: pour-the-cream-in-the-middle
    heading: Cream without a spoon
    text: >-
      We used to pour the cream on top of a full glass and then go looking
      for a spoon. Now we pour half the cold brew, add the cream, and finish
      the pour, and that last swish mixes the cup with nothing extra to wash.
  - slug: pantry-stockpile
    heading: A backup in the pantry
    text: >-
      Since Covid we have kept a backup of the pantry staples we actually
      reach for, and when something moves from the pantry into the fridge it
      goes on the shopping list so the backup gets replaced. With the kids
      changing their minds about dinner, that buffer means we are not dashing
      out for a single bottle of ketchup.

Weeknights in our kitchen start from whatever we can get going without a long setup. Eggs still cook the way Mads's dad always did them, in a quarter inch of water under a lid. Bacon can go into a cold oven while one of us showers, and a pound of ground beef we forgot to pull that morning can soften in a bowl of warm tap water before the evening gets away from us.
