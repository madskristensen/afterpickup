When the server comes over to our table, the kids know the first part is theirs, and they start the same way every time, smile, make eye contact and say "May I please have," followed by whatever they picked off the kids menu. That little moment is where our adventure tasks begin, somewhere around age five, because we want the kids to be able to talk to adults and find their way in the world without us doing all the talking for them. At five they are usually too shy, but slowly they come around, and one day they just order.

We have been out with other families where the parents order for the kids, which is completely understandable when everyone is hungry and the table is busy, and we noticed that the kids often did not look up at the server at all. Seeing that is part of why we keep nudging ours to do it themselves, even on the nights it would be faster to just say it for them.

When the boys were about seven or eight, Mads took them to a store, handed over a few bucks, and said he would wait right there at the exit while they went back in together, picked out an apple, and paid for it at the checkout counter on their own. They were excited to try it and a little nervous at the same time, and they came out so proud of themselves that first time.

From there the tasks grow with the kids. Somewhere around eight to ten, they go on bike rides or walks to places around the neighborhood they have not been before, sometimes with a map Mads printed out, and they learn how to get somewhere and back on their own. Around the same age, the two of them take their bikes and a backpack or tote bag to the store about half a mile away, buy something simple, and ride home with it, and sometimes they ride the same distance to Starbucks for a treat or a drink.

The first store run was apples and bananas, and the bananas came home brown all over. The boys were sure they had looked perfect and yellow at the store, and they had, but nobody had thought about bananas bumping around in a bag the whole ride home. You get those kinds of lessons too, and we count them right along with the wins.

Next on the list is ordering at a fast food place and bringing back the right order for everyone, which feels like a fair step up from one apple. It does seem like things are a bit more coddled for kids today than when we were kids ourselves, so we keep handing ours these small adventures, one step at a time, and each one gets them a little more used to dealing with adults and the real world.
