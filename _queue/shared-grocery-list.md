When Mads heads out to the store, he says the same thing on his way out the door every time, which is that if there is anything we need, just add it to the list. That works because our shared grocery list lives in the Reminders app on our iPhones, and he has it open the whole time he is walking through the store, so anything that gets added at home shows up on his screen pretty much right away.

We keep one list called Groceries for the regular store runs and for Instacart orders. We also have separate lists for Trader Joe's and Costco, because there are things we can only get at those stores, and it helps to have them collecting in one place until the day we actually go there.

We also keep a Vacation list for when we are staying at a vacation rental or hotel. On those trips we often like to pick up ice and some to-go travel cups for by the pool, and those are easy things to forget, especially while we are finding our way around a new grocery store with three kids in tow and because they are never on our normal shopping list.

Setting it up is simple. One of us creates the list in Reminders and shares it with the other, and from then on we both work off the same list. Every item either of us adds or deletes updates on the other phone almost instantly, so the list is always up to date and nobody is shopping from an old version of it. The only wrinkle is the odd store where our phones do not get reception, which is rare but does happen, so when Caitlin is headed to that store she takes a couple of screenshots of the list before she goes in, and that solves it.

Since we both wear an Apple Watch, a lot of the time we do not even reach for the phone. When we notice something is running low in the kitchen, or it pops into our heads in the car, we ask Siri to add it to the list right then, and it is there before the thought has a chance to slip away.

A shared grocery list inspired the first list, but now we share lists for plenty of other things, including the running list of ideas for this blog and the posts we want to write next. A shared list works for anything where you want to drop in an idea quickly and still be able to change it or take it off later, like a family bucket list. Once the first one is set up, it quickly becomes the way the two of us keep track of things together.
