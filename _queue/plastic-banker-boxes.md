Early on in parenting, Caitlin realized she was going to have to control her impulse to keep each and every work of art the kids made, otherwise we would be drowning in pictures of cars and rainbows. Using the container method (using a container to help manage how much of something you keep), each of our three kids has a [plastic banker box with hanging file folders](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09FVM5RNL/ref=nosim?tag=afterpickup-20){:rel="sponsored nofollow noopener" target="_blank"}. These boxes are where we keep art, keepsakes, notes from teachers, and class pictures. The idea is that each grade gets a file, and the goal is that age 0-18 fits in one banker box per kid. Each year as we add more, we're forced to review what we've kept in the past so that their baby books, drawings they've done, and the other cute little things they've made over the years will fit with future class photos, yearbooks, class projects, and papers.

What's really nice is that everything for each kid is contained in one box. We can stack them out in the garage, where they're out of the way. During the school year artwork and cool schoolwork gets tossed in a larger bin. At the end of each year Caitlin goes through the bin and selects the best of the best so that only the best is kept.

They come home every day and every week from school with a bunch of artwork and schoolwork and notes from teachers. We take the best stuff they bring home, and the cool things they're doing at home, and put it into the file for that grade. We also clean up, so we just keep the best stuff.

The idea is that when they turn 18, we're going to give them one file box that has every year in their life up to that point.
