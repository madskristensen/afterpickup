Last year, when our oldest turned ten, we skipped the bouncy house, the arcade, and the trampoline park and threw a card shop birthday party instead, at the Pokemon card and game store in town. We invited about eight of his friends, and it turned out that a minivan and an SUV were all we needed to fit every kid, so the whole party piled into two cars and headed out together.

The drive ended up being an event of its own. The kids took turns requesting music, phones got plugged into the car speakers, and the silly songs kept coming, so there was laughing the whole way there and the whole way back. We had not planned the car ride as part of the fun, but it was easily one of the parts everyone remembered.

At the store, we gave each kid ten dollars to buy a card, or anything else they wanted, that they could keep and take home. Not everybody went for Pokemon, since the shop had other kinds of cards and toys too, and that was part of what made it feel like their own pick. We were there about 45 minutes, and they really took their time, flipping through binders, comparing choices with each other, and changing their minds before settling on something.

Back home we had Costco pizza, and that was the whole party. Everyone was happy, and it was one of the most fun birthdays we have done and also one of the cheapest. A bouncy house runs roughly $500 to $700, and for a lot less than that the kids got a road trip with their own soundtrack, a shopping trip with their own money, and something to take home that they had picked out themselves.
