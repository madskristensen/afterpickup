The usual way to boil eggs in this house used to mean waiting. Fill a pot, wait for a lot of water to move, lower the eggs, wait some more. On an old slow stove that first wait is the whole afternoon. The kids want eggs after pickup. Nobody wants to watch a full pot sulk its way to a boil.

We do it the way Mads learned from his dad. About a quarter inch of water in the pot. Any size pot. Bring that little bit to a boil. It does not take long. Lower in however many eggs you need. They do not even have to touch the water. Close the lid. Start the timer.

Most methods start with a pot of water. Cold-start means you fill it, wait for the whole thing to boil, then keep going. Dropping eggs into a rolling boil still asks you to heat a full pot first, and the water cools when the eggs go in, so the clock is a guess. A quarter inch is already steam. Small batch or a pile of them, the wait is the same shallow boil.

Times from the lid going on. Soft-boiled: six minutes for small eggs, six and a half for large. Almost hard, still a soft center: nine minutes small, nine and a half large. Hard-boiled: ten minutes, or ten and a half for large. That is the whole list.

Lid on. Timer on. Eggs off. It is fast because you never wait for a pot of water, and it comes out the same every time.
