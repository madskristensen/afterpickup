The kids have been helping set the table since they were four, and the first job is clearing the table. There are often [art projects](/craft-mats/) on it, or other things left there, and they start by getting those out of the way so the table is ready for dinner.

While they do that, we set out the plates, the utensils, and the sauces from the fridge, like ketchup, up on the kitchen counter, which is easy to do from the kitchen. Then the kids come pick it all up and distribute it out on the table. They go to the drawer for the [cloth napkins](/black-bar-towels/), and they set those out too, because they already know how that works. They set the plates, and they put the knife and the fork on top of the napkin next to each plate.

As they get older, they can handle the warm dishes we put up on the counter, like rice or pasta, including the trivets that have to go underneath. We teach them how to carry warm things and how to balance them, and to look so that no one is coming who might bump into them while they are walking with a heavy pot of food.

Afterwards they all help clear their own plates. When they are four or five, they start by taking their own plate to the sink, and as they get older we tell them not to stop moving things off the table over to the kitchen sink until the table is clear. That includes taking the cloth napkins and putting them in the washing machine, and all the kids do that.

We give them more and more of the jobs that go with setting the table as they get older, but they start when they are four.
