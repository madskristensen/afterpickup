All of our kids have spent their little years running around the house barefoot, and none of them ever wanted socks, so we keep spare socks in a small bin right in the shoe drawer by the front door. The five-year-old is no different from her brothers at that age, and when it is time to leave the house she is almost always padding around with bare feet, happily unaware that socks have to come before shoes.

Her room and her dresser are upstairs, which used to mean that the moment we were ready to head out, and often already a little late, someone had to run up and dig out a pair of socks. This time of year it is not something we can skip, so the whole family would stand by the door waiting while the socks were found, and it rarely went smoothly.

The kids do not want to run to their room in that moment, and they can feel that they are the one holding everybody up, which is a hard feeling when you are small. More than once that turned into tears at the front door, and a stressed parent on the stairs, right when we all wanted to be in the car.

So when each of the kids reached the age where socks were optional in their minds, we started keeping a few extra pairs down in the [shoe drawer in their entry cubby](/kids-entry-cubbies/). Their regular socks still live in the dresser upstairs, and the spare ones sit in a frosted white plastic bin in the corner of the drawer, right next to the sneakers and boots. When it is time to go, she opens the drawer, grabs a pair of socks, puts them on, and the shoes go on right after.

That small bin has completely fixed the sock scramble for us. There is no running upstairs, no crying by the door, and no feeling that she is the reason we are late, and it saves us a surprising amount of stress on the way out of the house. When the socks in the bin run low, we bring a few more pairs down with the clean laundry, and the drawer is ready for the next time we head out the door.
