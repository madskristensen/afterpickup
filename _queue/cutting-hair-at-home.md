Whoever's getting a haircut sits on the wooden barstool, and Caitlin already has the plug-in clipper in her hand, ready to cut our family's hair at home. The scissors, a comb, and a neck brush are there on the seat too.

It started during COVID, when Caitlin first began cutting hair for Mads and the kids, and then for her mom too. It took a lot of trial and error. She was terrified the first time she tried to cut Mads's hair, and it didn't turn out well, so he had to go to the hairdresser and have it fixed. That was actually a mechanical issue. Mads has short hair, and we tried to use his beard trimmer for the edging and the neck, which was the wrong tool, and it didn't go so well.

She's been cutting our hair ever since, and the thing that finally made it work, for the adults and the kids in the house, was getting a few pieces of equipment that are actually meant for this. First is a proper trimmer, one you plug in. They're not very expensive, and they're so much better than a beard trimmer or whatever else you've already got around. They're purpose built for cutting hair, they go through it very easily, and it's very easy to avoid mistakes with a tool like that.

The other thing was a couple of proper scissors. One is a regular pair of hairdresser scissors. Caitlin is left-handed, and left-handed ones are, weirdly enough, more expensive, though they're still not that expensive, and that was helpful. The other pair has teeth, and those are really good for bangs and for when you want natural looking edges, so the hair doesn't look so boxy and weird.

Those few simple tools are really all it takes. We sit on the wooden barstool in the photo, Caitlin cuts, and afterwards we just vacuum around it. It's very simple, and she's been cutting our hair for years now, and it's going really well.
