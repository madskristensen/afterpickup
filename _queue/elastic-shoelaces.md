When our boys were little, getting shoes on at the front door was a quick rip and press of the Velcro straps, and off they went. Nobody had to learn to tie a bow back then, because nothing on their feet needed tying, and on a busy school morning that suited all of us just fine. Elastic shoelaces came later, once Velcro stopped being an option.

As they got older they learned to tie their shoes, which is a good thing to know, but somewhere along the way the shoes changed on us too. Around age eight to ten, most of the sizes they wear stop coming with Velcro, so the cool shoes they actually want to wear often only come with laces.

Knowing how to tie a bow is not quite the same as getting a nice snug fit, though. Some shoes are just tricky to lace, because the way they are made makes it hard to pull the laces nice and tight, and kids' fingers and hands do not have that kind of strength yet. We could tell the boys were working hard at it, and the shoes still ended up looser than they should be.

So we still buy the shoes with laces, and then we swap the regular laces out for elastic shoelaces we buy on Amazon. They thread through the shoe like any other lace, and instead of ending in a bow they run through a little toggle that holds the elastic in place. From then on the boys just slide a foot in and tighten the elastics with the toggle, with no bow and no knot to tie.

It gives us the Velcro feel with the laces look, and it has been really helpful for the boys, because they get to pick the shoes they want without the struggle of tying them. When the smart speaker announces [it's time to put on shoes](/its-time-to-brush-teeth/), they slide their feet in, give the toggles a pull, and they are ready to head out the door.
